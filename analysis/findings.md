# Workforce Analysis – Key Findings

## 1. Departmental Turnover

Sales recorded the highest turnover rate at 50%, followed by Operations at 20%.

This suggests that these departments warrant closer investigation into employee retention.

## 2. Employee Tenure

The 2–4 years of service group recorded a turnover rate of 14.13%, compared with 9.01% among employees with five or more years of service.

The under-two-year group recorded 100% turnover, but contained only two employees. This sample is too small for a reliable conclusion.

## 3. Overtime and Turnover

Employees in the high-overtime category recorded 20% turnover, compared with 5% in the low-overtime category.

This indicates an association worth investigating further, although it does not establish that overtime causes employees to resign.

## 4. Business Recommendations

- Investigate the reasons for high turnover in Sales.
- Review workload distribution and overtime practices.
- Examine employee retention during the two-to-four-year service period.
- Conduct exit interviews to identify recurring resignation reasons.
- Monitor departmental turnover regularly.

## 5. Data Limitations

All records are simulated. Findings are preliminary and reflect the assumptions built into the dataset. They should not be treated as real organisational evidence.
